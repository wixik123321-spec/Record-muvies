# Video editor

Turn raw footage into a finished edit: pick the moments worth keeping, plan the edit, get the user's approval, render with ffmpeg. Claude cannot hear audio or judge humor reliably, so the user rates the picks and the sound; every rating is logged and used to improve the rules.

## Setup and checks
Run `python -I scripts/doctor.py` on a new machine (ffmpeg with libass/x264, Python packages, fonts, models; it installs and downloads nothing). Titles and captions use the bundled open-licence fonts in `assets/fonts` (Kanit, Playfair Display, Anton; `VIDEO_EDITOR_FONTS=system` keeps the older Leelawadee/Georgia/Impact look) through `scripts/fonts.py`, so an edit looks the same on every OS. After changing any script run `python -I tests/run_tests.py` (10 regression tests on generated media, ~20 s); fix every FAIL before showing the user a render.

## Workflow
0. **Several clips? Order them first.** Run `scripts/order_clips.py` on the files: it reads Apple creation date, container creation_time, the date in the file name, then file modified time (weakest), and flags clips with missing or shared timestamps and overlaps. Show the proposed order and ask the user to confirm; recording order is not always the story order. Only when timestamps are missing or flagged, fall back to content cues (on-screen clocks, light from day to night, the same outfit or place, words like "yesterday" or "next") and say how unsure you are. Content-only ordering has not been tested.
1. **Inspect.** `ffprobe` for duration, resolution, fps, audio. Get a transcript: YouTube captions via yt-dlp when they exist; otherwise Creative Claw `transcribe` (ElevenLabs Scribe, about 2 credits for 2 minutes, character timings even for Thai; tell the user the cost first) or Whisper if installed (ask before installing; Whisper small fails on Thai). With a known script, time it with `scripts/align_script.py`. For long footage, get per-second loudness too.
2. **Find the beats (long or raw footage).** Keep only what a viewer wants: storytelling, interaction with people, showing something, punchlines; drop dead air and aimless walking (`references/highlight-criteria.md`). Run `scripts/find_beats.py` on the captions + loudness to get candidates, then look at frames of the top ones and read their text before choosing. The script mainly ranks dense dialogue, so treat its picks as candidates.
3. **Read the mood.** Classify each beat from speech and sound first, then visuals (`references/mood-lessons.md`); do not trust captions that mention things not on screen, and do not predict the user's score, ask for it.
4. **Pick a style** if not obvious and read its row in `references/style-recipes.md` (starting numbers from sample clips, not rules). For a podcast or interview Short start with `scripts/podcast_short.py` (one command, three approval gates: segments, B-roll downloads, preview; see `references/podcast-shorts.md`). For a product/ad, a screen-recording tutorial or an interview/podcast Short, follow `references/genre-recipes.md` (shot finder, beat grid, screen zoom, reframing, word captions).
5. **Write an edit plan and wait for approval:** per beat the time range (start at the setup, end after the payoff or reaction), subtitle notes, music yes/no. Cut beats together without dead air.
6. **Render.** For footage edits write the approved plan as one JSON file and render it with `scripts/edit_plan.py` (clips, transitions, Thai-safe titles, voice, ducked music, effects, subtitles, loudness; format in `references/edit-plan.md`); run `--preview A B` first. For Pillow photo animations render frames on all cores with `scripts/render_frames.py` (about 6x faster than one process) and encode through it. Otherwise use `references/ffmpeg-recipes.md` or `scripts/make_vertical_short.py` to export in 9:16, 16:9, or 1:1 with optional subtitles and music. Use `scripts/add_music.py` to score an already rendered clip. Work from low-res proxies for long footage. Never overwrite source files.
7. **Check and hand over.** Run `scripts/qa_check.py out.mp4 --subs subs.srt --target youtube|shorts|doc --sheet sheet.jpg` (format, colour range, loudness, true peak, black/frozen picture, silence, subtitle timing and width) and look at the sheet; fix every FAIL and explain every WARN. Check subtitle frames, say what could not be verified (the sound itself), send files and ask for ratings. Before a rating round, make a calibration pair if a level is in doubt (music 20 dB under speech was inaudible).

## Music policy (confirmed by the user)
- Narrated documentary or 2.5D animation: a soft music bed under the whole narration, following the mood (see `references/music-rules.md`). The rules below are for vlogs and live streams.
- While someone talks to the camera or in conversation: no music.
- Music fades in only where nobody talks: scenery, places, things the camera is pointing at, camera pans. It fades out about 0.5s before talking returns (timestamp ducking from the transcript, not a compressor alone; compressor only as a fallback).
- Dialogue-driven scenes (talking in a car or boat) rated lower with music; lively scenes with a matching genre (bossa in a restaurant) rated highest. Choose genre from the setting.
- Gap bed about 8 dB under speech loudness, fades 1-2s; details and evidence in `references/music-rules.md`. Raw chat-driven streams have almost no gaps, so expect little or no music there.
- Match style loudness: -13 to -15 LUFS for cinematic/Shorts, -18 to -19 for talk-led vlogs and storytelling; true peak below -1 dBTP (AAC adds about 1 dB, leave margin).
- Music and sound files: use the user's own or tracks licensed for the edit and retain their credits (see the workspace `music/CREDITS.md` pattern).

## Subtitles
Make an `.srt` from the transcript; when the words are known (a script, or a corrected transcript) use `scripts/align_script.py`, which cuts cues on real character times; for YouTube captions `scripts/vtt_to_srt.py` accepts WebVTT timestamps with or without hours and cleans up auto captions. Let the user check the text (Thai errors are common), burn in with `subtitles=`. Short lines (about 42 characters, 2 lines), on screen at least 1s.

## Vertical, Shorts and Reels
Hook within 2 seconds. When a webcam overlay exists, consider stacking the face and screen. The export script defaults to 9:16 (1080×1920). Use `--aspect 16:9` for 1920×1080 or `--aspect 1:1` for 1080×1080 with the same timing and captions. Default framing preserves the full image over a blurred background. Use `--framing crop` to fill the output with a center crop after checking that the subject stays in frame.

Export a 10-second Short starting at 2 seconds in a source video with no audio track:

```bash
python -I scripts/make_vertical_short.py footage.mp4 - 0 2 10 short.mp4 --music music.mp3
```

`-` skips subtitles. Omit `--music` to leave a video with no audio track silent. For a video with an audio track, pass its WebVTT captions when adding music so the soundtrack can duck during speech.

## Animation (3D, motion graphics)
The wanted style is 2.5D motion graphics built from **real photos** (cut-outs of the real place, people, animals, sky) layered with parallax and synced to a narration; drawn geometric stand-ins were rejected. Find licensed photos (Wikimedia Commons), cut out with rembg, compose frames in Pillow, keep a credits file. Rules that took the test clip from 6/10 to 8/10 (passed): every element is a real photo, the sky too; smooth sub-pixel motion; camera shake and sound effects only where the story is tense or exciting; fill the frame with depth layers (near large and bright, far smaller and hazed toward the sky); figures never cover the hero subject. Never crop through the hero subject: run `scripts/check_cutout.py` on every cut-out and fix any subject that touches a border unless that is intended (landscape bands). The v4 rules (sub-shots every ~3 s, word-level sync, one colour grade with paper texture, 1080p, moving atmosphere, puppet motion, depth of field, meaningful transitions, real sound, whooshes, explainer graphics, preview first) scored 9.5/10 on the Pompeii clip; they are listed in `references/animation-2-5d.md`. Earlier note: next-level rules being tested (sub-shots every ~3 s, word-level sync, one colour grade with paper texture, 1080p, moving atmosphere, puppet motion, depth of field, meaningful transitions, real sound, whooshes, explainer graphics, preview first) are listed in `references/animation-2-5d.md` with the method, scripts and pitfalls. Narration from other creators is for local tests only. For the voice itself (the user's recording, an AI voice through Creative Claw/ElevenLabs, or free edge-tts), the Thai number-spelling rule, and swapping the voice of a finished edit without re-tuning it (`scripts/retime.py`), read `references/narration.md`.

## Honest limits
- Claude cannot listen. Music fit, mix balance and humor need the user's ears. Its score predictions were not better than guessing 3 (mean error 0.92 vs 0.75), so do not promise taste.
- Caption-based tools depend on auto captions (errors, no tone); scene detection misses soft transitions; style rows come from few clips.
- Before downloading external media, confirm the user has authorized the download and the source offers a permitted download for the intended edit. Record the creator, source URL, license and changes with the deliverable.
